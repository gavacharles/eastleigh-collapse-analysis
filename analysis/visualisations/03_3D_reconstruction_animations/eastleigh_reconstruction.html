Sequence

  1. before 0Before the video. Rubble, ladders and partly missing blockwork are visible at L0 when filming starts; their purpose is unknown. Ground-floor shops were occupied without an occupation certificate (Governor). Cracks were reportedly seen earlier.
  2. 0–0.8 sCorner failure. L0 columns or walls at the street-corner end lose capacity. The column line above sags: the L1 slab end drops about 0.6 m relative to the centre (measured).
  3. 0.4–0.8 sZipper. Load shifts to the neighbouring L0 columns, which fail in turn along the frontage. The order comes from the frame model; the timing is schematic.
  4. 0.7–2.3 sGlobal onset. The superstructure drops almost vertically as one rigid block. L0 is crushed and air and dust jet out sideways. Resistance is about 0.4 of the falling weight.
  5. 2.3–5.5 sCrush-up. Storeys are crushed from the base against the growing rubble. Speed peaks at about 11–12 m/s, then resistance reaches the falling weight and the descent stops at the debris pile.
